        How to play

        Onigokko is tag. One player is the oni – they are “it”. Touch someone and it passes to them. Every little picture below is the real game, playing itself.

        1Tag

        The oni runs a touch faster, and whoever was just tagged cannot tag straight back. In Hot Potato, the oni at the buzzer loses. In Least Time, the least time spent as the oni wins.

        2Run and jump

        Hold jump longer to go higher, and press it again in the air for a second jump. Down drops you through thin ledges, and makes you fall faster.

        3Slopes

        Walk up and down them. They work like thin ledges: jump up through them from below, hold down to drop through or to pass underneath.

        4Trampolines and launchers

        A trampoline throws you straight up – in a real arena, far higher than in this little picture. A launcher, marked with arrows, flings you up and across. Steer in the air.

        5Updrafts

        A column of rising air. Step in, let go, and ride it up; steer out at any height. You get your second jump back inside.

        6Portals

        Walk in and you fly to the matching portal as a spark, in an arc over the arena. Nobody can tag you on the way. Land in one off a jump or a pad and it waits a blink – step away and you stay. Portals with an arrow mark let you drop out mid-flight: press jump.

        7Thorns

        They do not tag you, but they sting: a short stun and a bounce off them – just enough for the oni to catch up.

        8Lucky boxes

        Touch a box with empty hands to draw an item, then set it off when it suits you with E with the item button.

          9Team Bomb

          Two sides. Flags: there are three – A at one end, C at the other and B high in the middle. Stand in a flag's ring for seconds and it turns your colour; it stays yours until the other side stands there long enough to turn it. Bombs: one for every two players. Touch an enemy whose hands are free to stick yours on them. Points: what counts is the buzzer – every flag gives the team whose colour it is , and every bomb goes off and gives the other team . Until then only a little moves, to keep everyone at it: every seconds a flag gives its team and a bomb takes off the team holding it (a number pops up where it happens).

          Controls

          • ←→ or AD Run
          • ↑ W or Space Jump – again in the air for a second jump
          • ↓ or S Drop through thin ledges and slopes, fall faster
          • E X or Shift Use the item in your hands
          • O The round's objectives (they fold away by themselves)
          • Esc Pause menu
          • Left thumb strip: run. Slide across it to turn without lifting your thumb.
          • Big button: jump – press again in the air for a second jump.
          • Arrow-down button: drop through thin ledges and slopes, fall faster.
          • Item button: lights up when you hold an item; press it to set the item off.
          • Menu button (top right): pause, sound, leave.

          On a phone or tablet you get a thumb strip for running and buttons for jump, down and the item.

          Keyboard shortcuts

          • ↑↓ Move through any menu
          • ←→ Change the choice you are on (game, style, stage)
          • Enter Choose – in a room it starts the match
          • Esc Back – during a match it opens the pause menu
          • C P 0–9 Title screen: host a room, look through the open rooms, type a room's code
          • N Title screen: your name
          • G R N In a room: Quick Play or a tournament, which game, how many rounds
          • S A T In a room: next style, next stage (with Shift: previous); in a tournament, T picks which round they are for
          • L P In a room: round time, lucky boxes
          • − + B In a room: how many seats, and a bot in every open one (or every bot out)
          • C V In a room: copy the invite link, make the room public or private
          • X Z Team battle, in a room: change sides; the host shuffles the teams
          • H Menus: how to play
          • M F Anywhere: sound on or off, full screen

          Rooms

          • A room has 2 to 10 seats. Bots take the ones nobody has, give theirs up to anyone who walks in, and come back when someone leaves.
          • The host decides seat by seat: take a bot out, put one in, add or remove seats, send a player out.
          • With the stage or the style on Random, the tick on each picture says whether it is in the draw.
          • Stages come in two sizes: the small ones are for 2–6 players, the big ones for 5–10. One that does not suit the room is shut.

          Tournament

          • Knockout: Hot Potato, round after round. Whoever is the oni at the buzzer is out and watches the rest; the others play on, until one is left standing. The stages get smaller as the field thins.
          • Team Bomb tournament: 3, 5 or 7 rounds, the teams staying together. The host chooses how it is decided: Rounds won (whoever wins a round gets a point, a drawn round gives nobody one, and the last round is worth two) or Total points (everything adds up, the last round double). Level at the end? A tie-break round is played, until one side is ahead.
          • Every second round has a twist.
          • With the stage or the style on Random, a new one is picked before each round.
